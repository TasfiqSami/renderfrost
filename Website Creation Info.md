\## 05 — SITE-WIDE CUSTOM CURSOR SYSTEM



This is an essential feature, not an optional decorative suggestion.



Build a professional custom cursor system that feels integrated with the RenderFrost identity. It should be precise, exceptionally smooth, lightweight, and intuitive.



It must provide distinct behavior depending on where the pointer is positioned.



\### A. Default pointer appearance



On compatible desktop devices, create a custom visual pointer that may combine:



\- A small, precise central dot.

\- A refined outer ring.

\- An optional minimal frost-inspired crosshair treatment.

\- Smooth but immediate tracking.

\- Subtle transitions when moving between element types.

\- A quiet purple or frost accent.



The crosshair should be minimalist and elegant, not a gaming reticle with unnecessary lines and decorations.



The design must match the overall website rather than appearing as an unrelated extension.



\### B. Custom crosshair behavior



When the pointer moves over ordinary non-interactive background space, show the default crosshair or cursor appearance.



The crosshair should track the real pointer accurately.



A secondary ring may use a slightly eased animation, but the main pointer must remain responsive and should not visibly trail far behind.



Do not make the cursor lag by hundreds of milliseconds.



Do not allow the visual cursor to drift away from the actual click location.



Click targets must remain easy to interact with.



\### C. Clickable element behavior



The cursor must visibly respond when entering:



\- Navigation links.

\- Buttons.

\- Clickable text.

\- Interactive cards.

\- Clickable image panels.

\- Dropdown triggers.

\- Tabs.

\- Accordions.

\- Copy-IP actions.

\- Music controls.

\- Interactive icons.

\- Other genuine actionable elements.



Show a recognizable clickable pointer state, potentially using a refined custom arrow/pointer, a ring transformation, or an appropriate accent treatment.



The transition should feel smooth and intentional.



Clickable elements must also receive a proper hover, focus, and active visual state. A cursor animation is never a replacement for clear interaction feedback.



\*\*Important:\*\* Apply the clickable pointer to elements that actually perform actions. Do not label non-interactive text as clickable or make decorative cards appear actionable.



For actual links and buttons, preserve correct native semantics and expected browser behavior.



\### D. Text-selection cursor behavior



This behavior is essential.



When the pointer enters selectable text, paragraphs, headings where text selection is expected, legal content, rules, code-like server addresses, or ordinary text inputs, switch to the appropriate text-selection cursor.



Use the native text-selection/I-beam behavior where required.



The custom crosshair must not remain over selectable text in a way that interferes with reading or selection.



Allow visitors to select, copy, and highlight text normally.



Do not disable text selection globally.



For inputs and text areas, preserve the correct native text-editing cursors and browser interaction.



The custom pointer must never prevent text selection, hide a necessary I-beam, or introduce an invisible interaction layer over the document.



\### E. Cursor behavior by element type



Create a centralized, predictable cursor-state system.



Use explicit styling or semantic attributes where needed instead of fragile JavaScript guesses about what the user is hovering.



Correctly distinguish:



\- Ordinary page background: default crosshair treatment.

\- Actionable link or button: clickable pointer treatment.

\- Clickable card: clickable pointer treatment.

\- Selectable text: normal text-selection cursor.

\- Text input: native text-editing cursor.

\- Disabled control: appropriate disabled cursor/state.

\- Resize handles or draggable controls: correct corresponding cursor where applicable.

\- Busy or unavailable action: an appropriate state without falsely implying clickability.



\### F. Smooth implementation



Use an efficient approach with appropriate pointer events and request-animation-frame scheduling if needed.



Avoid unnecessary React renders, excessive DOM manipulation, and high-frequency layout recalculations.



Clean up listeners and animation loops.



Use appropriate stacking order so that the custom cursor never blocks clicks, text selection, focus indicators, or overlays.



The custom cursor layer should not intercept input events.



\### G. Compatibility and accessibility



On mobile and other coarse-pointer devices, disable the decorative custom cursor and preserve the natural touch experience.



Provide a graceful fallback for unsupported browsers, reduced-motion preferences, and devices where custom cursor tracking is inappropriate.



Keep native keyboard focus indicators.



The website must remain usable with a normal system pointer if the custom implementation fails or is disabled.



Do not force a custom cursor on visitors who cannot meaningfully use it.

Implement a gentle soundtrack introduction.

Use a sensible configurable sequence, for example:



\- Begin at 0% volume.

\- Gradually rise to a quiet introductory level over approximately 1–2 seconds.

\- Continue to a moderate preferred volume over the following several seconds.

These timings and volume levels are adjustable implementation defaults, not rigid requirements.



The increase must feel like a natural atmospheric entrance, not an abrupt loudness boost.



Avoid sudden changes, clipping, harsh peaks, or unnecessarily loud default volume.



\### Browser autoplay restrictions



Do not attempt to bypass browser autoplay protections.



If playback is blocked, present a polished, non-intrusive music control so the visitor can begin playback through an explicit interaction.



Do not continuously retry playback, generate console errors, or interrupt the browsing experience.



Never make essential website functionality dependent on music.



\### Music continuity



Keep audio in a persistent application-level controller where the existing architecture supports client-side navigation.



Do not restart the soundtrack every time the visitor opens another page.



Do not create duplicate audio players.



Handle browser refreshes, track loading failures, route changes, and tab visibility gracefully.



Do not assume that autoplay permissions automatically carry over to future browser sessions.



\### Volume control



Create a compact, premium music control integrated into the navbar.



Include:



\- Play/pause.

\- Mute/unmute.

\- Volume adjustment.

\- An accurate playing or paused state.

\- Smooth fade behavior.

\- An optional compact volume popover.

\- Clear accessibility labels.



The control should remain understated.



The slider must support 0% through 100%.



When muted, playback must become inaudible and the muted state must be reflected accurately.



When unmuted, restore the appropriate configured volume smoothly.



Do not display a playing animation when playback is blocked, paused, or unavailable.



\### User preferences



Remember visitor preferences such as volume and mute state using an appropriate client-side storage mechanism where justified.



Do not store unnecessary personal information.



If the visitor manually changes the volume during the initial fade, respect the new preference instead of unexpectedly forcing the volume upward afterward.



User control takes priority over the automatic fade sequence.



\### Audio failure



If the track is unavailable or unsupported, the site must continue working normally.



Disable or hide the unavailable control appropriately and handle the problem gracefully.



Do not leave loading indicators running indefinitely.



Do not add unnecessary audio visualizers or CPU-intensive processing.

\### Authoritative rule set



\*\*1. Be Kind \& Respectful\*\*



Hateful speech, slurs, harassment, excessive vulgarity, and intentionally abusive behavior are prohibited. Reports involving abusive or inappropriate behavior will be taken seriously and may result in moderation action.



\*\*2. No Griefing or Stealing\*\*



Stealing, destroying, or intentionally damaging another player's property is prohibited. Reported incidents may be investigated, and confirmed violations can result in appropriate consequences.



\*\*3. No Unconsented PvP or Player Harm\*\*



PvP is disabled by default. Trapping, lava or dripstone attacks, killing, or intentionally harming another player without their consent is prohibited.



\*\*4. No Hacks, X-Ray, or Duplication\*\*



Unfair clients, modifications, exploits, and tools that provide unfair advantages are prohibited.



Examples include X-Ray texture packs or mods, Meteor Client and hacked clients, seed crackers, resource locators, and duplication exploits.



Litematica and Tweakaroo are listed as allowed modifications, provided they are used responsibly and do not provide an unfair advantage.



\*\*5. Rollback Policy — Destruction\*\*



Rollbacks are considered for destruction or loss caused by verified server-side problems, such as severe lag, crashes, or server-related disconnections. Player-caused destruction may be considered when confirmed griefing is involved.



Normal player- or entity-caused destruction is not eligible for compensation.



\*\*6. Rollback Policy — Inventory Loss\*\*



Inventory restoration may be considered when item loss is directly caused by a verified server-side issue, such as severe lag, a crash, or an unexpected disconnection.



Natural deaths, accidental deaths, and mob-related deaths are not eligible for inventory restoration. Players who believe their loss qualifies should report it to staff.



\*\*7. Do Not Mine Straight Down\*\*



Mining directly downward can result in falls, lava deaths, and hazards affecting other players. Mining straight down is prohibited and is a punishable offense.



\*\*8. Finish Chopping Trees\*\*



Remove the entire tree, including remaining logs. Do not leave partially chopped trees, and help maintain the world’s appearance.



\*\*9. Do Not Modify Spawn\*\*



Spawn Village is a protected area. Players must not destroy, build over, remove, or modify it without explicit staff permission.



\*\*10. Elytra Limit\*\*



Each player may own a maximum of three Elytras. Hoarding or trading additional Elytras is not permitted.



New players receive an Elytra after 30 days of joining as a gift from the staff team.



\*\*11. Channeling Trident Restrictions\*\*



Do not use Channeling-enchanted Tridents near player bases, houses, or other important structures in ways that cause damage or disruption.



Damage caused as a joke is not automatically repaired. Confirmed intentional griefing may result in punishment.



\*\*12. Building Guidelines\*\*



Players can build creatively but must avoid excessively lag-inducing farms, machines, redstone systems, or structures.



Maintain reasonable distances between large builds when necessary to preserve server performance and the world environment.



\*\*13. Whitelist \& Player Invites\*\*



RenderFrost uses a whitelist. Only staff members can officially add players.



Do not invite or add players without informing staff first. Players who want a friend to join must contact a staff member and follow the verification process.



\*\*14. Respect Storage Areas\*\*
